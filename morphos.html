    Tap as an attack comes in, from any side: your whole body hardens for about half a second and takes much less damage. A block that catches a hit refunds stamina. With the Barbed shell mutation it also throws part of the blow back.
    W · E · A · S · D
    Venom volley, Pounce, Whirlpool, Swarm and Decoy, one for each class. Each appears when something in the pond wakes its class: twelve kills (Pounce), three new cells (Swarm), three venom stings or venom kills from jellies and puffers (Venom volley), five close calls or rams on predators at speed (Whirlpool), twelve seconds hidden near a predator (Decoy). They start at novice strength; using them trains their class.
    Hold right click
    Swim fast while stamina lasts. Let go before it empties: an empty bar locks every stamina action until it refills. You can't eat while swimming fast. Swimming fast into a creature rams it for damage, and bounces you off.
    Hold or tap Space
    Sneak to stay unseen: hold, or tap to toggle. Attacking or swimming fast reveals you. Ambushes from hiding hit hardest and teach Stalker. An ambush only counts against something that has not noticed you. Once you hit it, it knows where you are until you hide and it loses track of you: quickly if you are far away or it is chasing one of your decoys, slowly if you stay close. Every attack, ambush or not, also hits harder the further round a creature or boss you strike: up to 15% more on its side and 30% more from straight behind.

    Controller
    Left stick swims, right stick aims (and turns you on the spot). RT strikes, LT swims fast, LB blocks, RB sneaks (hold, or tap to stay hidden). Abilities: A Venom volley, X Pounce, L3 Whirlpool, Y Swarm, B Decoy. Start pauses, View opens navigation. In menus: d-pad or left stick moves, A selects, B goes back, LB/RB switch tabs.
    Touchscreen
    Steer with the thumbstick that appears under your left thumb; strike, block, swim fast and sneak with the buttons under your right thumb. Strikes fly where the stick points, or the way you face when it rests; tapping open water strikes towards that spot. The DNA icon opens your build.

    Evolution. You have five classes, and each one trains from its own actions, never from eating. Predator trains when your Jab, Bite and Pounce land and kill. Venomist trains when your venom does damage. Brood trains when your broodlings fight. Riptide trains on rams, near misses and escapes while swimming fast. Stalker trains on ambushes. When a class's experience fills, it gains a rank on its own, and each rank in a class needs more experience than the last, so focusing on one class takes it deep and using several grows them side by side. At ranks II and IV a class forks: choose one of two specialisations on the Lineage tab whenever you like (play carries on), and the other closes for this life. Rank V is each class's capstone. After it, experience grows mastery. Raise two neighbouring classes to rank II and a hybrid forms between them. See it all on the Lineage tab.

    Divisions. Eating builds growth. When growth fills, your colony divides: one more cell, a larger body, more health, and a stat point for the six core stats. Each division raises the stat rank limit, up to rank 10; from 31 cells every stat can reach rank 12. You can refund them at full health and stamina, outside elder encounters.

    Mutations. Six body parts each hold one mutation, all open from the first cell. Earn mutations through play, from rare creatures, elders and ancient fossils, and swap them on the Mutations tab whenever no apex predator is hunting you.

    Water levels. The pond has a fixed difficulty that depends only on where you are. The nursery is level 1, and the water gains a level about every 850 units you swim out from it. Everything living in water is made for that water's level: its size, its health, its bite and how much its food is worth. Your own level is your cell count. The line under your health shows the water's level, in amber when it is above yours and red when it is three or more levels above. Shallow water stays easy however big you grow, and gives little; deep water is dangerous until you have grown into it. New species first appear at their own depths.

    The pond changes as you descend. Glass spires begin to stand among the plants from water level 9, clusters of tube-dwellers from 15 and the ribs of old giants from 21; each grows commoner the deeper you go. Two kinds of place exist only in deep water. A glass vault (from water level 12) is guarded by a warden, an ancient of that water. Bring the warden down, then wait at the vault's centre: it opens and gives up a mutation as good as its water can drop, Rare at the least. A black smoker (from 18) pours out minerals between its vents: feed there to claim it.

    Ancients. Out in the open water, a few old creatures hold territories. Each has a name, a gold halo and a health bar, about four times the health of its kind, and a harder bite. It cannot swallow you, and it will not follow far from home, so you can always leave. While you are hurting it, it will follow you anywhere; leave it alone for a few seconds and it goes home and slowly recovers. Once it falls it does not return. Bring one down and it always drops a Rare or better mutation.

    Levels. Every creature has the level of its water. Each level between you and it changes the damage you deal to it and take from it by 8%, and what it is worth by 12%. Places you investigate pay according to their water too.

    Better finds in deeper water. How good a dropped mutation can be depends on the water it drops in: Uncommon at best in Sunlit water, Rare in The Reaches, Epic in The Glass Fields, and Legendary from The Deeps down. An ancient drops one tier better than its water allows.

    Your Strike. Every cell starts with a plain Jab. Weapons are mutations for the Strike organ, dropped by the creatures that use them: Jaws from hunters (Bite: short range, the hardest hit), a Spitting gland from puffers (Spit: medium range, poisons), a Needle spine from needlefish (Needle: long range, a quiet shot), a Spike battery from skimmers (Spike gun: medium range, hold to fire a stream of small spikes) and a Spore sac from swarmers (Spore burst: short range, a wide spray of spores). A better copy of a weapon hits harder. Your classes decide your abilities, not your Strike.

    Predator

    Chase, bite and kill. Pounce on prey, devour small creatures whole and build Frenzy by killing in quick succession.

    Venomist

    Stack poison with Spit and Venom volley, then rupture heavily poisoned creatures so the venom bursts into their neighbours.

    Brood

    Bud daughter cells that follow you and attack whatever you strike. Swarm sends them all at your aim.

    Riptide

    Swim fast, ram and dodge, and spin up a Whirlpool that holds smaller creatures. A lunge or shot that only just misses you gives Momentum: speed and harder rams.

    Stalker

    Sneak near real predators and land concealed ambushes. Hiding beside harmless food does not count. Grow camouflage and decoys.

    Watch the five experience bars on the body screen to see which class ranks up next. Mixed play creates mixed creatures. This is a playful interpretation of evolution.

    Explore restorative blooms, sheltered sanctuaries, gene caches, predator nests and fast currents. Sanctuaries heal after you have shaken pursuit and avoided damage for a few seconds. Complete research to record your progress and earn score. Eating algae or protein restores an extra 1.5% of maximum health each second for six seconds. More food refreshes the duration without stacking the rate. This works everywhere, including during attacks and boss fights. Predators pursue and commit to lunges. They keep swimming while recovering stamina. Watch their movement, and choose when to turn, accelerate, conceal or fight. Smaller organisms seek vegetation; armoured scavengers gather around remains. Healthy predators remain dangerous even when they are smaller than you. Beyond five ranks, a class gains mastery.

    The apex predators.

    Six apex predators wake at levels . When you reach one's level it picks up your scent and comes for you, wherever you are; sanctuaries do not stop it. You can lose it by getting well clear of it, or by staying hidden until it loses track of you, and it comes back two minutes later. Grow past it and it hunts harder: one level over, it is faster and harder to lose; two or more, it never lets you go.

    It heals only from what it catches in its mouth. Poison stops most of that healing and bleeding halves it. Every attack is announced by a marked line or area: get out of it, then strike while it recovers, when it takes extra damage. At two thirds and one third of its health it changes and gets more dangerous, and below a quarter it is enraged.

    Defeating one gives its genome, a unique mutation and 2 stat points. If it kills you, you return to your last checkpoint and it comes for you again.

    Automatic checkpoints are set when you begin a life, unlock a gene cache, nutrient bloom, predator nest or sanctuary, finish resting safely at a sanctuary, or defeat an apex predator. Location checkpoints retain your current health and stamina; unlocking does not grant a free heal. Simply discovering a distant location or riding a current does not save. At a sanctuary, recover your health and stamina, then settle for 3 seconds to set one checkpoint for that visit. Open the map with Tab or the minimap to track a nearby sanctuary or one you have discovered. Attacking briefly suspends its shelter; let your attacks settle before resting. Ordinary travel, evolution and pausing do not change your checkpoint. Use Continue on the title screen or Return to checkpoint after defeat. Loads pause until you are ready. Your checkpoint place and time appear beside your score. Starting a new life replaces that checkpoint. Save life from the pause menu to keep a separate file or move your creature to another device. Saves from earlier versions are converted when they are loaded.
