Cast in Stone: privacy

Last updated 28 November 2026

In short: Cast in Stone runs in your browser and keeps your progress on your own device. There are no accounts, no analytics and no advertising. Playing alone sends nothing anywhere: the game only fetches its own files from jza.app. Co-op connects your device directly to your friend's device, which means sharing your internet address with them and, briefly, with the services that help the connection start.

What is stored on your device

Your cave, your golem and your sound settings are saved in your browser's local storage on this device. They are not sent to us or to anyone else. Clearing your browser's site data for jza.app removes them.

Playing alone

The game makes no network requests while you play alone, apart from loading its own files from jza.app when you open it, and checking for a newer version: a few seconds after it opens, and when you come back to it (at most every five minutes), it fetches its own page from jza.app to read the version number, and offers a reload if there is a newer one. Nothing is sent.

Co-op

Co-op connects two players' browsers directly to each other. Each device runs the game itself; the only game data that travels between them is button presses, the host's cave when the game starts, and each golem's progress. None of it passes through our servers. Starting the connection involves the following.

  • The connection helper (coop.jza.app). To connect, two browsers must first swap a block of technical connection details, which includes your device's internet (IP) addresses. When you host or join with a code, each browser leaves those details with a small helper service we run on Cloudflare, and the other browser collects them. The helper deletes them as soon as they are collected, or after two minutes if they are not. It stores nothing else and keeps no record of who connected to whom.
  • Address lookup (STUN). To find out how your device can be reached from the internet, your browser asks public address-lookup servers run by Cloudflare and Google. They see your internet address and nothing about the game. They are governed by Cloudflare's and Google's privacy policies.
  • The other player. Because the connection is direct, the person you play with can learn your internet address, as with any direct connection. Play co-op with people you trust.
